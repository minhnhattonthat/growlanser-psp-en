# Growlanser: Translator Synopsis

Full spoilers. Based only on the supplied summaries. Chapters below are editorial groupings, not official chapter titles. Scene files often contain multiple visits, alternate outcomes, and later dialogue; IDs are references, not a numerical play order. Battle failures do not establish canonical deaths. Speech notes describe broad tendencies; preserve each line's immediate context.

## 1. Characters and voices

| Character | Identity, relationships, and speech |
|---|---|
| Hero `{s:0000}` | Player-named adopted son of Sandra and older brother of Luise. Dreams, exceptional strength, and mysterious origins drive the story. Ultimately revealed as Gevel's human replica of Berger. Responses range from considerate to boastful or cruel; do not impose one fixed personality. |
| Tippi | Sandra's fairy-shaped homunculus, assigned to monitor the hero. Cheeky, blunt, jealous, and quick to kick him; increasingly tender when his life is threatened. Calls Sandra “Master.” Her short lifespan matters late. |
| Luise | Sandra's daughter, a total-solar-eclipse Growsian and the hero's adoptive sister. Shy, affectionate, earnest; becomes determined when protecting others. Calls him “Big Brother,” never “big bro.” Her romantic feelings and fear of abandonment develop through optional events. |
| Sandra | Rolandia's Court Magician, adoptive mother to the hero, biological mother to Luise, creator of Tippi, and former pupil of Wenzel. Calm, polite, analytical, and maternally reassuring. Researches Grosh in magic crystals. |
| Wallace | Veteran mercenary and mentor; lost both eyes and his right arm, relying on Sandra's magical prostheses. Searches for his former captain, Berger. Gruff, practical, restrained, with dry humor and paternal concern. Former comrade of Weber and Gamlan. |
| Ariost | Half-Featherian researcher raised in Boulogne; Gina is his mother. Builds a flying apparatus to reach her. Curious, explanatory, persistent, and socially awkward; believes research should help people. Loves Misha in relevant branches. |
| Misha | Luise's best friend at the Magic Academy; exuberantly infatuated with the hero. Clumsy, romantic, imaginative, and persistent despite academic struggles. Maxwell secretly created her as a human-sized homunculus with fabricated memories. Her crisis needs emotional sincerity beneath the comedy. Translate kinship address terms rather than retaining “Onii-sama.” |
| Xenos | Proud swordsman and mercenary supporting Karen. Berger's biological son, ultimately crucial to saving the world. Direct, competitive, fiercely protective; resentment and coercion draw him into the Shadow Knights. Also an accomplished cook. |
| Karen | Gentle healer and nurse, grateful to Irene. Raised alongside Xenos as his sister; later revealed to be Berger's stepdaughter, unrelated to Xenos by blood. Modest and caring, but capable of resisting her brother's possessiveness. Her romantic uncertainty concerns both Xenos and the hero. |
| Elliot | Sheltered boy resembling Richard; actually Angela's original son and rightful Burnstein heir. Fourteen during the main adventure, fifteen in an ending. Initially hesitant and dependent, later compassionate and increasingly assured as king. Admires the hero as an older-brother figure. |
| Richard | Gevel's replacement for the infant prince; gifted Imperial Knight Master, then king. His genuine friendships with Oscar and Ernest coexist with Gevel's control. Regal and forceful under domination; vulnerable and conflicted when himself. Later depends on Wenzel to survive. |
| Julian / Julia Douglas | Lord Douglas's daughter, presenting as male to enter the Imperial Knights. Formal, disciplined, proud, and protective; struggles with paternal approval and duty against friendship. Private romantic branches use “My Lord.” Keep “Julian” where the script does, even after disclosure. |
| Oscar Reeves / Ernest Lyell | Imperial Knights and Richard's close friends. Oscar supports Elliot while seeking Richard's recovery; Ernest remains loyal to Richard. Both speak with martial formality. Their rivalry retains warmth, respect, and grief. |
| Alcadius / Leticia / Angela | Alcadius is Rolandia's responsible, formal king; Leticia is his sheltered daughter, eager for friendship. Angela is **Burnstein's Queen Mother** in the supplied narrative: Elliot's birth mother, who also raised Richard and loves him. Her dialogue combines royal dignity with maternal conflict. |
| Berger / Sierra | Berger is Wallace's missing commander, Xenos's father, Karen's stepfather, and the hero's biological template. A rebel against ancient Growsian rule, displaced through time. Resolute and protective. Sierra, associated with his past and the ring, appears as a sorrowful ghost; do not explain her recognition of the hero prematurely. |
| Stella / Gina | Stella is the Featherian queen; initially rational, proud, and withholding, later accepting shared responsibility. Gina is Ariost's mother, punished for loving a human; thoughtful, affectionate, and knowledgeable about the Power Stone. |
| Maxwell / Bradley | Maxwell, Academy headmaster and Misha's guardian/creator, conceals cruel ambitions behind scholarly authority. Bradley, initially suspicious-looking vice-headmaster, genuinely researches curse removal and becomes a vital ally and later headmaster. Learned, sometimes boastful or comic. |
| Wenzel | Sandra's teacher and former Burnstein court magician, first presented as a helpful witness. Actually the ancient Growsian king fused with Gevel. His cultivated authority becomes openly contemptuous, absolutist, and cruel. |
| Gevel / Jung / Masked Knights | Gevel is a biological weapon seeking human domination; Jung are lesser creatures used against people. Masked soldiers include Gevel's replicas. Gevel speaks imperiously and threateningly; do not treat every creature or masked figure as the same individual. |
| Gamlan / Grengal / Oswald | Gamlan, Wallace's former comrade, uses covert violence, hostages, and curses. Grengal prolongs war for weapons profits and sometimes assists the party for that purpose. Oswald is a recurring hired bandit. Their roughness differs from royal and scholarly registers. |
| Weber / Bronson / Bernard | Weber is Wallace's former comrade and Ranzak's general; Bronson commands at Fort Rajin; Bernard leads the Nostridge forces. Practical military authority, with concern for troops and civilians. |
| Irene / Nick | Irene is a Growsian sanatorium worker and Karen's benefactor; Nick is her devoted protector and lover. Her loss of Grosh and memory anticipates Luise's crisis. Their eventual reunion offers a quieter personal resolution. |

Use natural modern fantasy English, without thee/thou. Keep glossary spellings and hyphenated honorifics. Preserve every name token exactly. The glossary's placement of Angela in Rolandia conflicts with these summaries; do not silently transfer Burnstein's royal family to Rolandia.

## 3. Branches, endings, and optional events

- **Relationships:** Leave, gifts, responses, bedside visits, fireworks, and final private conversations develop different companion bonds. Main clusters: s013, s020, s093, s101, s147–s154, s237–s261. Julian's duel and private pledge are in s154/s234. Do not combine every confession into one simultaneous romance.
- **Karen:** Captivity and the curse can lead to rescue or death. The rescue uses Bradley, Karen's hair, holy soil, and a substitute doll (s033, s041, s047, s203–s204). Later Xenos dialogue, revenge scenes, and endings change accordingly.
- **Misha:** Reassurance can restore her ability to fight during Maxwell's confrontation; later recovery involves the hero, Luise, or Ariost (s195, s248). Ariost's love and a possible marriage ending are separate from the hero's romance.
- **Endings:** s011 contains romantic outcomes with Luise, Misha, Karen, Julian, and Tippi, plus companion-focused outcomes for Wallace, Ariost, Xenos, and Elliot. Tippi has short-life and extended-life variants; her apparent death can lead to a surprise reunion in a human-sized body.
- **Other optional content:** Estate development and recruited artists/cooks (s067, s147); arena Free Battles (s044, s111, s231); Dungeon Man's cave and paid rewards (s056, s067, s145); Rosaria errands; gifts and baths; hospital patient care. The girl with Pechi may recover or die (s038–s039, s048).
- Civilian survival changes thanks, rewards, celebrations, and grief. Exhibition victory over Julian is conditional. Defeat cries and failed escorts usually describe failure branches, not additional story events. The summaries do not establish exact affection thresholds or route requirements.
